Robert, one idea I had was to train an ML model to replicate Paulette's MD. It would predict a final atomic configuration given an initial atomic configuration, not the whole simulation. This would allow us to approximately search through the space of MD simulations looking for one which best fits the experimental data (solve the inverse problem). Then Paulette could run a reliable MD simulation on the resulting initial atomic configuration, giving us a detailed lens into what the radiation did. I just did an estimate for this job on the CascadesDB dataset, which is an MD dataset focused on collision cascades, developed by the International Atomic Energy Agency, asking Opus 4.6 to estimate it, including time to swap into memory 1-2 atomic configurations for the larger 10M+ atom configurations and it estimated 150,000 AUs for a sequence of 20 short runs, 10 medium runs and 3 production runs.
